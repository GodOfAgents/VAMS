# VAMS remediation mission

## Outcome

Repair and evidence a restricted, faucet-only Polygon Amoy plus Cardano Pre-Prod deployment of VAMS. Preserve modular service implementations and the user's Heart-Brain architecture: safety constraints govern composition, service execution, verification and economic settlement. Do not turn this into an asset-management product or merge the separate Self-Evolving Harness project.

The audit baseline is 829ab544571f12efe50ecb5740f129c8acb3e16f. Current source, observed behavior and appropriately authenticated evidence determine findings. Documentation, this kit and the historical audit can contain mistakes; resolve disagreements through tests and explicit rationale.

## Scope authority

Default release scope remains the dual-host internal profile. Creating an intermediate local/rehearsal stage does not remove Cardano from the target. A narrower deployment, different bridge trust model, provider substitution, altered governance/economic invariant or new safety exception requires a reviewed scope/architecture decision. An explicit non-authoritative lab relay must never be represented as a cryptographic bridge verifier.

Finish all in-scope engineering. Keep broader GCA representation/calibration research and unsupported integrations on a visible backlog. GCA-compatible interfaces and pre-effect safety tests are required in the remediation target; a claim of GCA enforcement requires its stronger end-to-end evidence. Do not advertise universal alignment from an enum, policy hash, PCA distance or ZK circuit.

## Working agreement

Local reads, narrowly scoped repairs, tests, synthetic fixtures and local commits on a dedicated repair branch are the intended work. Do not push or open publicly revealing issues without authorization. Install tools or access networks only through approved routes. Do not assume a tool approved once permits unrelated operations.

Repair loop: hypothesis -> failing native regression -> minimal repair -> passing regression -> relevant full gates -> separate review -> evidence-linked disposition. Make small coherent commits. Revalidate after merge/rebase, because evidence for an earlier tree is not final-tree evidence.

## Do not manufacture green results

No deleting assertions, broad skips, xfail conversion, lowered thresholds, fake receipts, always-true verifiers, manually inflated audit status, hidden suppressions or skipped stage checks. A legitimate obsolete-test replacement needs proof that the replacement preserves or strengthens the property and explicit review.

Keep baseline test counts and newly added tests distinguishable. Any false-positive audit finding needs a reasoned counterexample and regression evidence, not compliance with the audit at the expense of correctness.

## Trust and key boundaries

Do not possess deployment signing material during repair work. The sandbox may allow reads outside the repository; .gitignore is not access control. Use a dedicated development account/distro with no valuable keys, avoid mounted sensitive Windows folders, and keep signing in a separate user-controlled environment. Do not ask the user to paste tokens or private keys. Scan and sanitize logs before publishing.

Keep emergency pause/recovery operable under narrowly scoped authority when external anchors are unavailable. Policy loss must block new protected effects, not force unsafe loss of recovery control.

## Completion reporting

Every end-of-session handoff names: branch/commit; changed files; findings reproduced/fixed/disputed/blocked; exact command exits and test counts; artifact location/hash; unresolved risks; reviewer required; next task. Use NOT_RUN and BLOCKED_EXTERNAL truthfully.

The final result is a release-candidate evidence pack, remaining external prerequisites and a specific deployment/rehearsal approval request. Codex does not self-authorize the broadcast or certify independent assurance.
